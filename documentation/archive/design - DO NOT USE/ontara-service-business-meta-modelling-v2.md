## 1. Purpose and Intent

This document describes the Business Meta Model (BMM) of the [[ontara-project-map|Ontara]] platform — a structural vocabulary that captures what any service business *is*: its value proposition, financial logic, resource strategy, activity structure, governance obligations, and strategic direction. The BMM is one half of a [[principle-two-meta-model-distinction|two meta model architecture (A4)]]; the other half is the Business System Meta Model (BSMM), which captures how a business *system* works.

The BMM addresses three goals:

- **Make the business model explicit and visible** alongside the business system model, so that the two can be developed iteratively, with each informing the other.
- **Define a generic vocabulary** that captures the structural, functional, and dynamic features of a service business model — applicable to any business that sees itself primarily as serving customer need. Healthcare specifics arise only when a specific domain (such as GenderSense Limited) instantiates the generic abstractions.
- **Enable quantitative exploration** of business model variants through parameterised scenarios, financial projections, and operational steering — turning a structured description into an explorable, computable tool.

The two meta models are distinct but connected by explicit, visible mappings. A ServiceOffering in the business model maps to one or more service processes in the system model. A RevenueStream maps to billable events generated by the delivery system. A ResourceType maps to workforce, equipment, or platform elements. These mappings are where the iterative back-and-forth between business model and business system happens — and making them structural (rather than implicit) is a core benefit of the approach.

Under the [[ontara-discussion-domain-identity-architecture-2026-03-22-v2.1|multi-tenancy principle (A13)]], only the meta model is core. Every domain — including the primary motivating use case, GenderSense Limited — is a tenant instantiation: an exercise of the platform's capabilities against a specific service business. The BMM vocabulary is designed to generalise across structurally different service domains, and this generalisation is validated through three demonstrator domains (§5).

The intended benefits are: completeness checking (has every concern been addressed?), modular exploration (can I substitute this component and trace the consequences?), stress-testing (does this business model variant work financially and operationally?), and communicability (can I discuss this with a non-technical colleague, an investor, or a clinical advisor by pointing at a visible structure rather than narrating from memory?).

## 2. Conceptual Framework

### 2.1 The Six Concerns of a Service Business

Any service business, regardless of sector, must address six orthogonal but interconnected concerns. These are not layers or stages — they coexist and interact continuously. Each concern is realised as a named package within the BMM.

**Service Concept** (`BusinessModel::ServiceConcept`) — What value is delivered, to whom, and why it is worth paying for. This encompasses the value proposition, the customer segments served, the service offerings packaged for delivery, the channels through which customers are reached, the differentiation claims that distinguish the service from alternatives, and — critically — the identification of [[concept-service-subject|service subjects]] (what the service acts upon) and [[concept-service-participant|service participants]] (who else is involved, in what role). It answers: "why does this business exist, and for whom?"

**Activity Model** (`BusinessModel::ActivityModel`) — A cross-cutting foundation beneath and across all other concerns. The business must be aware of every unit of activity that occurs within it, whether that activity is system-implemented or human-implemented, whether it is directly billable or not. Activity is the common currency that connects the concerns: the Service Concept promises value; the delivery system produces it through activities; resources are consumed by activities; the Financial Model can only produce meaningful projections if it can see the full activity picture. Activity awareness is treated as a concern in its own right (rather than merely a cross-cutting theme) because it has its own vocabulary, its own progressive elaboration model, and its own planning and costing structures. See §2.4 for the full treatment.

**Resource Planning** (`BusinessModel::ResourcePlanning`) — What resources and capabilities are required to operate the service delivery system, and how they are acquired, developed, and allocated. Resources include people (by role and competence), estate and facilities, equipment, technology, licences, and external services. Capabilities are the organised combinations of resources that produce service outcomes. It answers: "what do we need in order to do what we do?"

**Financial Planning** (`BusinessModel::FinancialPlanning`) — How money flows through the business. Revenue streams (how income is generated from service delivery), cost structure (how resources consume money), unit economics (the relationship between a unit of service delivered and its financial impact), pricing models, and financial projections linked to scenarios. It answers: "does this work financially?"

**Governance Mapping** (`BusinessModel::GovernanceMapping`) — How governance obligations are tracked and evidenced. This concern provides the structural vocabulary for governance traceability: what the obligations are (GovernanceRequirement) and what records demonstrate compliance (AuditEvidenceRecord). The traceability chain runs from requirement through testable condition (domain-specific constraint def) through formal satisfaction link to proof of compliance. This concern package was added in Session 42 when the [[domain-suds|Suds]] laundry demonstrator required COSHH governance traceability, validating that [[principle-clinical-governance-first-class|governance as a first-class concern (A8)]] is not healthcare-specific but a general service business need.

**Strategy and Evolution** (`BusinessStrategy`) — Where the business is heading. Strategic objectives with timeframes and success criteria, business model variants for exploration, and pivot scenarios for structured what-if analysis about transitioning between variants. This concern connects upward to enterprise strategy and downward to the scenario modelling mechanics that parameterise each variant.

The broader governance ecosystem — regulatory requirements (`Enterprise::Regulation`), governance processes (`ServiceDelivery::ClinicalGovernance`), risk registers, and learning mechanisms — spans both the BMM and the BSMM. The GovernanceMapping concern within the BMM provides the general vocabulary for the traceability chain; domain-specific regulatory content and governance processes live in the system model packages where they are operationally managed.

### 2.2 Relationships Between the Six Concerns

The concerns are not independent. The key structural relationships are:

- The **Service Concept** defines what the **delivery system** must produce and what **governance obligations** attach.
- The **delivery system** consumes **Resources and Capabilities** to operate, generating **activities** as it does so.
- **Resources and Capabilities** have costs, which flow into the **Financial Model**.
- **Activities** are the common currency: the Service Concept promises value; the delivery system produces it through activities; resources are consumed by activities; the Financial Model can only produce meaningful projections if it can see the full activity picture.
- **Governance Mapping** constrains all other concerns — regulatory limits on what can be offered, how it can be delivered, who can deliver it, and what can be charged. Governance requirements may also impose resource constraints.
- The **Financial Model** constrains what **Resources and Capabilities** can be acquired, which constrains what the delivery system can do, which constrains what **Service Concept** can be realised.
- **Strategy and Evolution** provides the intentional direction that the other concerns serve, expressed as strategic objectives that trace through to the capabilities required to achieve them.

This creates a circular dependency that is not a defect — it is the reality of running a business. The BMM makes the circle visible and navigable rather than implicit and surprising.

### 2.3 Modularity Principle

Each concern is decomposed into components. Components have defined interfaces (what they require from and provide to other components), substitutability (a component can be replaced with an alternative that satisfies the same interfaces, allowing exploration of business model variants), and varying levels of coupling — loose coupling at higher abstraction levels (the Service Concept does not dictate platform technology choices), tighter coupling at lower levels (a specific clinical pathway requires specific clinical competencies).

This is the "loose fit at high abstraction, tight fit at low levels" principle. The BMM defines the interface contracts; the concrete model implements them.

### 2.4 Activity Awareness — A Cross-Cutting Foundation

Beneath and across all six concerns lies a foundational requirement: the business must be aware of every unit of activity that occurs within it, whether that activity is system-implemented or human-implemented, whether it is directly billable or not.

This matters because activity is the common currency that connects the concerns. If any category of activity is invisible, the financial model underestimates cost, the resource model underestimates demand, and the capacity model produces false confidence.

**Activity taxonomy.** Every activity in the business falls into one of five categories:

- **Service delivery activity** — directly produces value for a customer. Visible to the customer, typically the basis of billable events. Examples: conducting an assessment, reviewing blood results, grooming a dog, processing a laundry order.
- **Service-enabling activity** — supports service delivery but is not directly visible to the customer. Essential for quality and continuity. Examples: case preparation, inter-professional communication, equipment maintenance.
- **Governance activity** — satisfies compliance, quality, and safety requirements. Mandated by regulation, professional standards, or internal governance. Examples: clinical audit, COSHH risk assessment review, GDPR record-keeping.
- **Development activity** — builds future capability. Invests current resource to create or improve future capacity, skills, or systems. Examples: CPD, platform development, service design, staff training.
- **Overhead activity** — necessary operational activity that is neither directly value-producing nor capability-building. Examples: financial administration, insurance renewals, premises management.

These categories are not rigid boundaries — a single activity might serve multiple purposes. The taxonomy exists to ensure completeness of coverage, not to force artificial classification. The important thing is that nothing falls through the gaps.

**Progressive elaboration.** Activity awareness operates at multiple levels of granularity, and different parts of the business model and system model can operate at different levels simultaneously:

- **Envelope level** — total activity hours per category estimated as a lump. Suitable for early-stage business model exploration where precision is neither possible nor necessary.
- **Category level** — activity broken down by type within each category, with approximate proportions and durations. Suitable for more detailed resource planning and financial modelling.
- **Tracked level** — individual activities are recorded by the system as discrete events with participants, duration, resource consumption, and category classification. This is what the business system provides when fully operational.

Different parts of the business can operate at different granularity levels at the same time. Clinical service delivery might be at the tracked level from day one (because the pathway model already generates observable events). Governance activity might be at the category level. Overhead might remain at the envelope level until there is a reason to be more precise. Over time, granularity increases where it adds value — typically where a cost turns out to be larger than expected, or where a resource constraint turns out to be tighter than assumed.

**Relationship to projections.** When running financial or resource projections, every projection draws on activity data. At the envelope level, projections use estimated percentages and averages. At the tracked level, projections use actual recorded data. The cross-cutting nature of activity awareness means that a projection can mix granularity levels and produce a result that is as precise as the available data allows, with the imprecision explicitly visible rather than hidden.

## 3. The Business Meta Model Vocabulary

The BMM vocabulary is defined in SysML v2 as `part def` and `requirement def` elements, each annotated with comprehension metadata (§4). The vocabulary is distributed across three SysML files to manage file size, but logically forms a single coherent model.

### 3.1 Core elements (28, in `BusinessModel`)

The core BMM package contains 28 elements across six concern packages. All 28 are classified as General — common to most service businesses, sector-agnostic. No Tailored (sector-specific) BMM elements have been introduced to date; the current vocabulary has proven sufficient to model four structurally different service domains.

**Service Concept** (9 elements):

| Element | Type | Description |
|---|---|---|
| CustomerSegment | part def | A defined group of customers with shared needs and willingness to pay |
| ValueProposition | part def | A promise of value to a customer segment — what they get and why it matters |
| ServiceOffering | part def | A packaged unit of service that can be priced, resourced, and delivered |
| Channel | part def | A way that customers find, reach, or receive the service |
| DifferentiationClaim | part def | An explicit, testable statement of how the service differs from alternatives |
| ServiceSubject | part def | The entity upon which a service is performed — person, animal, object, or organisation |
| ServiceParticipant | part def | An entity involved in a service engagement in a defined role |
| CatalogueEntry | part def | A record of something the business has decided to offer, with price and status |
| ExternalReference | part def | A link to authoritative knowledge that lives outside the system |

ServiceSubject and ServiceParticipant were introduced in Session 55 to resolve a structural observation from the Paws demonstrator (Session 44): the entity upon which a service is performed (the dog being groomed) is not always the customer (the pet owner). These are sibling concepts, not a hierarchy — either can independently trigger workflow. The subject may have independent needs (animal welfare, clinical requirements, handling constraints) that override the customer's stated preferences. Governance obligations bind to the subject. Cross-domain validation confirms the distinction: Paws (dog/owner), GSL (patient/commissioner/carer), Suds (garments/customer), Cafe (customer is also the subject — a valid trivial case).

[[concept-catalogue-entry|CatalogueEntry]], [[concept-external-reference|ExternalReference]], and [[concept-inventory-record|InventoryRecord]] (in ResourcePlanning) implement the [[pattern-four-layer-item-model|four-layer item model]]: what something intrinsically is (item definition) → how the business offers it (catalogue entry) → its operational stock state (inventory record) → links to external knowledge (external reference). This pattern was validated in the [[domain-cafe|Coffee Shop]] Extension exercise.

**Activity Model** (5 elements):

| Element | Type | Description |
|---|---|---|
| ActivityType | part def | A defined kind of business activity, classified into one of five categories |
| ActivityRecord | part def | A recorded instance of an activity that actually happened |
| ActivityBudget | part def | A planned allocation of activity volume by type and period |
| ActivityGranularity | part def | A policy declaration of how precisely activity is tracked in a given area |
| ActivityCostAllocation | part def | The mapping from an activity type to its financial cost |

**Resource Planning** (7 elements):

| Element | Type | Description |
|---|---|---|
| ResourceType | part def | A category of resource needed to run the service — people, equipment, premises, or external services |
| ResourceInstance | part def | A specific resource available at a defined capacity and cost |
| Capability | part def | An organised combination of resources that produces a defined service function |
| CapacityModel | part def | The relationship between resource configuration and service throughput |
| ResourceConstraint | part def | A limit on resource availability that affects service delivery |
| InventoryRecord | part def | A point-in-time record of stock level for a catalogue item |
| ObjectiveCapabilityMapping | part def | A declared connection between a strategic objective and supporting capabilities |

ObjectiveCapabilityMapping provides strategy-to-operations traceability: from StrategicObjective (a requirement def in BusinessStrategy) through this mapping to the Capability usages that contribute to achieving it. This closes the loop between "what are we trying to achieve?" and "what resources and service functions make that possible?"

**Financial Planning** (5 elements):

| Element | Type | Description |
|---|---|---|
| RevenueStream | part def | A mechanism by which the business generates income from its services |
| CostDriver | part def | A structural factor that generates cost — linked to a resource or activity |
| UnitEconomics | part def | The revenue, cost, and margin for a single unit of service delivery |
| PricingModel | part def | How prices are set — per-episode, subscription, tiered, or other basis |
| FinancialProjection | part def | A forward-looking financial picture linked to a scenario definition |

**Governance Mapping** (2 elements):

| Element | Type | Description |
|---|---|---|
| GovernanceRequirement | requirement def | A regulatory or standards obligation that the business must satisfy |
| AuditEvidenceRecord | part def | A record that demonstrates compliance with a governance requirement |

GovernanceRequirement is modelled as a `requirement def` (not `part def`) to enable formal `satisfy` relationships to domain-specific constraint usages. The traceability chain: GovernanceRequirement → constraint def (testable condition, domain-specific) → satisfy → AuditEvidenceRecord (proof of compliance). This chain has been validated in the Suds demonstrator's COSHH governance model.

### 3.2 Projection and strategy elements (14, in `BusinessScenarios` and `BusinessStrategy`)

These elements provide the mechanics for driving the business model vocabulary — computing projections, comparing alternatives, stress-testing assumptions, and expressing strategic direction.

**Scenario Modelling** (11 elements, in `BusinessScenarios`):

| Element | Type | Description |
|---|---|---|
| ScenarioDefinition | part def | A named, self-contained configuration of a business model variant |
| ProjectionParameter | part def | A named, typed variable within a scenario |
| GrowthAssumption | part def | A time-parameterised trajectory for a variable |
| ProjectionFormula | part def | An evaluable expression connecting model components |
| ProjectionTimeline | part def | The temporal structure over which a projection is computed |
| ProjectionOutput | part def | The time-series result of evaluating a scenario |
| SensitivityParameter | part def | A parameter identified for sensitivity analysis |
| ScenarioComparison | part def | A structured side-by-side comparison of scenario definitions |
| PeriodActuals | part def | Actual financial and activity figures for a completed period |
| VarianceAnalysis | part def | Structured forecast-vs-actual decomposition |
| ForecastBaseline | part def | A versioned snapshot of a scenario's parameters at a point in time |

**Strategy and Evolution** (3 elements, in `BusinessStrategy`):

| Element | Type | Description |
|---|---|---|
| StrategicObjective | requirement def | A defined business goal with timeframe, success criteria, and capability traceability |
| BusinessModelVariant | part def | A named alternative business model configuration at summary level |
| PivotScenario | part def | A structured what-if analysis for transitioning between variants |

StrategicObjective is a `requirement def` to enable formal `satisfy` relationships to Capability usages in ResourcePlanning — preserving structural traceability for future tooling.

### 3.3 Total vocabulary summary

| Package | File | Elements | Classification |
|---|---|---|---|
| BusinessModel (6 concern packages) | business-model.sysml | 28 (26 part defs + 2 requirement defs) | All General |
| BusinessScenarios | business-scenarios.sysml | 11 part defs | All General |
| BusinessStrategy | business-strategy.sysml | 3 (2 part defs + 1 requirement def) | All General |
| **Total** | | **42** | **All General** |

The entire BMM vocabulary is currently classified as General. No Tailored (sector-specific) elements have been required to date. The General/Tailored decomposition ([[ontara-discussion-component-catalogue-model-assembly-2026-03-18|B11]]) provides for sector-specific extensions when needed — for example, a healthcare-specific `ClinicalGovernanceRequirement` extending GovernanceRequirement, or an education-specific `AccreditationRequirement`. The current vocabulary's generality is itself a validation finding: four structurally different service domains have been modelled without requiring sector-specific meta model extensions.

## 4. The Comprehension Architecture and the BMM

The BMM is not only a structural vocabulary — it is a self-describing vocabulary. Every element carries comprehension metadata that enables the system to explain what it contains, why it is structured that way, and how elements relate to each other. This is the comprehension architecture, the major achievement of Sessions 45–58, built directly on top of the BMM.

### 4.1 Self-describing elements

Every one of the 28 core BMM elements carries four annotation types:

- **`@CatalogueTag`** — identifies the element's BMM concern and General/Tailored classification. Used by the generation pipeline to route elements to the correct console views.
- **`@UserFacing`** — a friendly name and short description for non-technical users. The element's public face.
- **`@PurposiveDescription`** — a human-written explanation of why the element exists and what it does for the service business. Written in second person ("When you define your customer segments, the system uses them to..."). These are authored content — Register 1 in the three-register comprehension model.
- **`@Comprehension`** — four boolean flags (`surfaceEnumValues`, `surfaceDomainInstantiations`, `surfaceRelatedConcepts`, `surfaceAttributes`) that tell the generation pipeline's traversal discovery engine which structural facts to dynamically derive for this element. This is Register 2 — structural self-knowledge.

Coverage is complete: 28/28 for all four annotation types.

### 4.2 Weighted relationships

79 `@WeightedRelationship` annotations express the strength of interaction between BMM concepts. These relationships are the structural topology of the BMM — they answer the question "if this element changes, what else needs reassessment?"

Key properties:

- **Directional and non-commutative.** The weight on A → B answers "if A changes, how much does B need reassessment?" The reverse B → A is independently assessed. Weights do not net off or average.
- **Ordinal classification.** Strong (27), moderate (50), weak (2). AuditEvidenceRecord is a pure receiver with zero outgoing weights — it records evidence but does not structurally influence other concepts.
- **Rationale-bearing.** Every weight annotation carries a rationale explaining why the relationship has the assigned strength. These rationales are themselves comprehension content.
- **Five inductively established heuristics** (H1–H5) govern weight assignment, documented in the [[ontara-ref-weighted-relationship-heuristics-and-config|weighted relationship heuristics and configuration reference]].

The [[principle-unity-principle|unity principle (A11)]] commits the platform to a single [[concept-weighted-relationships|weighted relationship]] model informing comprehension, reasoning, simulation, governance, and assembly guidance. The same weights that drive the Glossary view's "related concepts" display will inform future inferential comprehension (Register 2+), governance impact analysis, and assembly workspace guidance.

### 4.3 The three-register model

| Register | Content | Source | Status |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Register 1: Authored** | Human-written purposive descriptions | `@PurposiveDescription` metadata | Complete. 28/28 coverage. |
| **Register 2: Structural** | Facts the model already knows — type, relationships, containment, patterns, domain instantiations | Dynamically derived via `@Comprehension` traversal schema | Complete. 28/28 coverage. Generator traversal engine operational. |
| **Register 2+: Inferential** | Derived explanations beyond what any single element states — analogies, gap analysis, impact propagation | Computed from weighted relationships, cross-domain comparison | Research direction. Depends on reasoning formalisms (M7). |

The [[principle-intrinsic-self-knowledge|intrinsic self-knowledge principle (A10)]] governs the boundary: if the model changes and no human edits a description, does the explanation become wrong? If yes, that content must be intrinsic — dynamically computed, not stored as static text.

### 4.4 The BMM in the Ontara Console

The [[concept-co-evolution|co-evolution principle (J2)]] requires that modelling and tooling advance together. The BMM vocabulary is made legible through multiple console views:

- **Coverage Matrix** — which BMM concepts are instantiated in which domains. The primary cross-domain validation view.
- **Component Catalogue** — four-quadrant classification (General/Tailored × BMM/BSMM) with domain instantiation status. Multi-axis grouping and comprehension layer rendering.
- **Glossary** — every BMM element with its authored purposive description, intrinsic comprehension content, and weight-aware related concepts displayed with a warm-to-cool dot bar indicator. Alphabetical listing, search, BMM Concern/Layer filtering, inline expand/collapse, and cross-links to the Component Catalogue and Coverage Matrix.
- **Governance View** — traceability from requirements through constraints to satisfaction evidence. The GovernanceMapping concern's traceability chain rendered navigably.

All four views are generated from a single `model-introspection.json` data source, itself generated from the SysML model by `gen_model_introspection.py`. This is the comprehension architecture in practice: the model describes itself, the generator extracts the descriptions, and the console renders them for human navigation.

## 5. Cross-Domain Validation

### 5.1 The demonstrator strategy

Demonstrator domains serve two purposes ([[concept-cross-domain-validation|J1]]): cross-domain validation (proving that the BMM vocabulary generalises across structurally different businesses) and pedagogical anchoring (concrete illustrations that make abstract concepts tangible for non-technical users).

### 5.2 The domains

| Domain | Character | Key structural difference | Regulatory tier | BMM vocabulary scope |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| [[domain-cafe\|Cafe]] | Immediate retail | Per-item pricing, walk-in, 2-minute cycle | Generally governed | Full model + running application (22 validated patterns) |
| [[domain-suds\|Suds]] | Batch processing | Weight/type-dependent pricing, batch turnaround, item tracking | Lightly regulated | Full BMM + COSHH governance traceability chain |
| [[domain-paws\|Paws]] | Appointment-based personal service | Per-appointment pricing, breed/size surcharges, persistent client/animal identity | Lightly regulated | General vocabulary only; ServiceSubject + ServiceParticipant instantiated |

GenderSense Limited (GSL) is the fourth domain and the primary motivating use case. Under the [[ontara-discussion-domain-identity-architecture-2026-03-22-v2.1|multi-tenancy principle (A13)]], GSL is the most important tenant — but still a tenant. Its distinction is purpose (production healthcare delivery) and regulatory tier (sector-regulated), not structural privilege.

### 5.3 Validation findings

The cross-domain validation programme has produced several concrete findings:

- **All General vocabulary has proved sufficient.** Four structurally different service domains have been modelled without requiring Tailored (sector-specific) BMM extensions. This is itself a significant validation result.
- **ServiceSubject and ServiceParticipant generalise.** The Paws demonstrator exposed a gap (the dog is the subject, the owner is the participant), which was resolved by introducing two sibling concepts that generalise cleanly across all four domains.
- **GovernanceMapping generalises.** The COSHH governance traceability chain in Suds validated that the GovernanceRequirement → constraint → satisfy → AuditEvidenceRecord pattern works for any regulatory regime, not only healthcare.
- **The four-layer item model generalises.** CatalogueEntry/ExternalReference/InventoryRecord validated across coffee menus, laundry item catalogues, and clinical formularies.
- **The four-tier regulatory classification works.** Generally governed (Cafe), lightly regulated (Paws, Suds), partially regulated (not yet instantiated), and sector-regulated (GSL) provide a clean discriminator for governance intensity without constraining the types of regulation that may apply.

## 6. Package Structure and the SysML Model

### 6.1 Three files, one logical model

The BMM vocabulary is distributed across three SysML files for file size management (the original single-file BusinessModel package exceeded 1,900 lines before Phase 6A split):

| File | Package | Contents |
|---|---|---|
| `model/business-model.sysml` | `BusinessModel` | ServiceConcept, ActivityModel, ResourcePlanning, FinancialPlanning, GovernanceMapping. 28 elements. Domain-specific instantiations (GSL Lean Clinical and Full Platform). |
| `model/business-scenarios.sysml` | `BusinessScenarios` | Projection mechanics: ScenarioDefinition, projections, sensitivity, variance, steering. 11 elements. Domain-specific scenario instantiations. |
| `model/business-strategy.sysml` | `BusinessStrategy` | Strategic direction: StrategicObjective, BusinessModelVariant, PivotScenario. 3 elements. Domain-specific objective and variant instantiations. |

`BusinessScenarios` and `BusinessStrategy` are separate top-level packages (not sub-packages of `BusinessModel`) due to a Syside constraint: same-named packages across files trigger global-namespace-distinguishability errors. The split is purely organisational.

### 6.2 Cross-references between packages

The BMM packages reference each other through typed `ref` attributes rather than string conventions:

- `ScenarioDefinition.activeServiceOfferings` → `ServiceOffering[0..*]` (cross-package ref)
- `FinancialProjection.scenario` → `ScenarioDefinition` (cross-package ref)
- `StrategicObjective.relatedScenarios` → `ScenarioDefinition[0..*]` (cross-package ref)
- `Capability.enabledServiceOfferings` → `ServiceOffering[0..*]` (same-file cross-package ref)
- `ResourceConstraint.affectedResource` → `ResourceType` (same-package ref)
- `InventoryRecord.catalogueEntry` → `CatalogueEntry` (cross-package ref)
- `CostDriver.linkedResource` → `ResourceType[0..1]` (cross-package ref)

These typed references were established through the [[session-58-report-2026-03-24|Session 58]] string-to-typed-ref migration ([[deferred-string-to-typed-ref-migration|O25]]), replacing what were previously String-typed attributes with formal SysML ref relationships. This strengthens structural traceability and enables generators and validators to follow the reference chain rather than parsing string conventions.

### 6.3 Relationship to Operations

There is an intentional division of responsibility between the BMM's financial and resource concerns and the Operations package:

- **`BusinessModel::FinancialPlanning`** — structural economics: how does this business model work financially? Revenue streams, cost drivers, unit economics. The bank-manager view.
- **`Operations::Finance`** — operational accounting: invoice processing, payment reconciliation, payroll, accounting integration. The day-to-day financial machinery.
- **`BusinessModel::ResourcePlanning`** — structural resource needs: how many clinicians for N patients? What capabilities are required?
- **`Operations::People`** — operational HR: contracts, recruitment, appraisal, indemnity.

The BMM asks "what does the business model require?"; Operations asks "how are those requirements managed operationally?" This mirrors the BMM/BSMM distinction at the concern level.

## 7. Mapping to the Existing System Model

The existing Ontara SysML model maps onto the BMM's concerns with varying degrees of coverage. This mapping shows where the system model already supports the BMM's vocabulary and where gaps remain.

### 7.1 Service Concept → Now modelled

The ServiceConcept package is fully elaborated with nine elements. ## 8. Business Model Variants and Scenario Modelling

### 8.1 Three business model variants

The BMM supports exploration of business model variants as evaluable scenarios. Three variants are defined for the GSL tenant:

**Variant A: "Lean Clinical"** — Assessment and hormone therapy only. No coaching, no community. One prescribing clinician (0.5 FTE stepping to 0.8), one admin (0.3 FTE), platform only. Per-episode pricing. Break-even target at ~50 active patients, estimated around month 14. Fully elaborated with ScenarioDefinition, parameters, growth assumptions, and projection formulas.

**Variant B: "Full Platform"** — Assessment, hormone therapy, coaching, education, community, patient portal. Two clinicians, one coach, one community moderator, full platform development. Monthly subscription model plus per-episode clinical fees. Target 200 active patients within 18 months. Higher investment (~£120K), longer break-even, higher revenue ceiling. Fully elaborated with ScenarioDefinition and financial instantiation.

**Variant C: "Consultancy + Platform Licence"** — Dual revenue: clinical services AND platform licence/SaaS to other gender services. Highest investment (~£200K), longest break-even, but platform revenue scalable independently of clinical capacity. Defined at summary level (BusinessModelVariant); ScenarioDefinition deferred.

### 8.2 Projection mechanics

The scenario modelling vocabulary provides the mechanics for computing projections, comparing alternatives, and stress-testing assumptions:

- **ScenarioDefinition** selects active service offerings, sets parameters, specifies growth assumptions, and configures resource levels. A scenario is a complete, evaluable specification.
- **ProjectionParameter** defines the adjustable variables per scenario — prices, FTE levels, growth rates, overhead percentages. Some are scalar, some temporal, some derived from other model components.
- **GrowthAssumption** defines time-parameterised trajectories: linear, stepped, S-curve, or custom. These bridge between a point-in-time business model description and a time-series projection.
- **ProjectionFormula** defines evaluable expressions connecting components — revenue formulas, cost formulas, utilisation calculations.
- **SensitivityParameter** identifies which assumptions the model is most dependent on. For the Lean Clinical variant, sensitivity analysis reveals that patient acquisition rate is the dominant variable: the difference between 2 and 6 new patients per month shifts break-even by approximately one year.

### 8.3 Operational steering: the forecast–actuals–rebaseline cycle

Once a business is operational, the projection becomes a living instrument. The cycle is:

1. **Establish baseline.** A ScenarioDefinition becomes the operational plan — ForecastBaseline version 1.
2. **Record actuals.** Each period, actual figures are recorded as PeriodActuals against the same structure as the projection — same revenue streams, cost drivers, activity categories, resource utilisation dimensions.
3. **Analyse variance.** VarianceAnalysis compares actuals to forecast, decomposed by cause: volume variance (fewer or more units than expected), price variance, mix variance, timing variance. Each variance is typed by a VarianceSource enumeration.
4. **Update the forward forecast.** Small adjustments update the current baseline without creating a new version.
5. **Re-baseline when necessary.** When evidence indicates that assumptions are materially wrong — not just noisy, but structurally incorrect — a new ForecastBaseline version is created. Parameters are updated, the forward projection re-run from the current position. This is a deliberate decision, not an automatic adjustment.
6. **Compare across baselines.** Three views are always available: original plan, current forecast, and actuals.

### 8.4 Architectural connection to self-knowledge

The forecast-actuals-rebaseline cycle is not a standalone capability. It is structurally parallel to the self-knowledge architecture already elaborated in `Knowledge::LogicEngine`, following the [[pattern-five-layer-self-knowledge|five-layer SystemStateAssessment]] structure:

| Self-Knowledge (Clinical) | Operational Steering (Business) |
|---|---|
| GoalProjector projects goals from requirements and constraints | ProjectionFormulas project financial goals from scenario parameters |
| OperationalStateAggregator queries current state | Extended aggregator queries financial actuals and activity records |
| GapAnalyser compares state against projections, produces Deficits | VarianceAnalysis compares actuals against projections, produces structured decomposition |
| ExplanationTrace provides structured "why" answers | Extended explanation provides "why is revenue below plan?" answers |

The BusinessScenarios package imports from `Knowledge::LogicEngine` and `Foundation::CommonTypes`, establishing the structural wiring between the business model and the self-knowledge architecture. GoalProjection and Deficit part defs from LogicEngine can express financial goals and variances, validating the architecture as domain-agnostic.

This means that implementing operational steering is primarily an extension of existing infrastructure — the same evaluation, inference, and explanation machinery that already serves clinical self-knowledge.

## 9. The Two Meta Models

### 9.1 Current state

The [[principle-two-meta-model-distinction|two meta model distinction (A4)]] is the load-bearing architectural commitment that separates *what a service business is* from *how a business system works*.

The **Business Meta Model** is now explicitly modelled (42 elements, this document) and navigable through the Ontara Console. Its vocabulary, comprehension metadata, and weighted relationships form a self-describing structural vocabulary for service business modelling.

The **Business System Meta Model** remains implicit — its concepts ([[concept-persistence-policy|PersistencePolicy]], [[concept-agency-classification|AgencyClassification]], GoalProjection, Deficit, and others) are distributed across Foundation, Knowledge, ServiceDelivery, Platform, Operations, and PatternCatalogue. A future workstream will promote these into a named, navigable structure. The existing package structure implies a system meta model; the work is to make it explicit and factor out the domain-specific content. This is tracked as [[deferred-system-meta-model-extraction|a recognised deferred item]].

### 9.2 The mapping structure

The two meta models are connected by explicit horizontal mappings at every tier ([[ontara-discussion-component-catalogue-model-assembly-2026-03-18|B12]]):

```
Business Meta Model (explicit — 42 elements)
├── Service Concept              — value, segments, offerings, channels, subjects, participants
├── Activity Model               — cross-cutting activity taxonomy and costing
├── Resource Planning            — resources, capabilities, capacity, constraints
├── Financial Planning           — revenue, cost structure, unit economics, projections
├── Governance Mapping           — traceability: requirement → constraint → evidence
├── Scenario Modelling           — projection mechanics, comparison, sensitivity, steering
└── Strategy and Evolution       — objectives, variants, pivots

    ↕ explicit horizontal mappings ↕

Business System Meta Model (implicit — distributed across 6 packages)
├── Service Delivery             — processes, episodes, entity lifecycles
├── Knowledge and Decision       — rules, constraints, decision support, learning
├── Platform and Technology      — infrastructure, integrations, data
├── Operational Management       — workforce, finance operations, reporting
├── Governance Mechanisms        — regulatory compliance, audit, risk
└── Foundation                   — cross-cutting types, patterns, metadata
```

Domain-specific models instantiate both meta models. A ServiceOffering in the business model maps to one or more ServiceProcesses in the system model. A RevenueStream maps to billable events generated by the delivery system. A ResourceType maps to workforce, equipment, or platform elements in the system model. A GovernanceRequirement maps to Enterprise::Regulation requirements and domain-specific constraint defs.

### 9.3 The General/Tailored decomposition

Within each meta model, components are classified as General (common to most service businesses, sector-agnostic) or Tailored (sector-specific, extending or specialising general components). Both tiers exist on both the business and system sides. Currently, all 42 BMM elements are General — no Tailored extensions have been needed. This decomposition is documented in the [[ontara-discussion-component-catalogue-model-assembly-2026-03-18|Component Catalogue discussion (B11)]].

## 10. Simulation: Practising the Business Before Running It

The BMM's structural vocabulary, combined with the scenario modelling mechanics and the system model's pathway and workflow infrastructure, brings a simulation capability within architectural reach. Simulation differs fundamentally from projection: a projection aggregates ("at month 12, you'll have approximately 50 patients generating approximately £X revenue"), while a simulation instantiates — generating individual simulated patients, each flowing through the pathway model, consuming specific resources, encountering specific decision points, and revealing emergent behaviour that aggregation hides.

This section describes the simulation capability as a future workstream. The key design concerns are:

### 10.1 Simulation data generation

A generative model produces realistic input data: patient generation (arrival rate from GrowthAssumptions, segment distribution, clinical profile distribution), event generation (lab results, GP responses, cancellations with realistic timing and content), and environmental starter sets (initial state configurations enabling different simulation scenarios — new launch, steady state, or replay from a historical point).

### 10.2 Workflow execution under simulation

The pathway action flows and their Temporal orchestration counterparts define processes through which simulated entities flow. In simulation mode: automated workflow progression (signal waits resolved by the event generator with configurable delays), decision point resolution (from simple rule-following to sophisticated clinical variation), and resource contention (simulated activities compete for finite resources, revealing wait times and scheduling pressure invisible in static analysis).

### 10.3 Temporal control

Simulation time is decoupled from real time: time compression (24-month scenario in minutes), start/stop/pause/save/delete lifecycle, variable time intervals (daily for scheduling effects, monthly for long-horizon scenarios), and checkpointing for branching ("what if, at month 6, we had hired a second clinician instead of raising prices?").

### 10.4 Simulation reporting

The simulation produces the same data structures as the live system: periodic reports using the same financial, activity, and governance summary structures; variance analysis against the ProjectionOutput from the same scenario; and cumulative outcomes (patient journeys, financial trajectory, resource utilisation patterns, bottleneck identification).

### 10.5 Purposes of simulation

Learning and intuition-building (what does it feel like to run a clinic with 40 active patients?), stress-testing (what happens if patient acquisition drops by half for three months?), demonstration (a running simulation with visual interface for investors, partners, or advisors), training (staff practise operating the system in a consequence-free environment), and evaluation (comparing simulation outcomes across variants provides richer basis than projection comparison alone).

## 11. Forward Direction

### 11.1 Information governance and cybersecurity (B20)

Information governance and cybersecurity have been identified as a cross-cutting concern that will eventually require representation in both the BMM and the BSMM ([[ontara-workflow-emergent-ideas-log|E011]], Session 62). IG is not one more regulatory obligation to tick off — it is a systemic property of how the platform handles information, consent, data flows, and threat surfaces. The scoping paper is a horizon item. The current BMM vocabulary (GovernanceRequirement, AuditEvidenceRecord) can accommodate IG-specific governance requirements as domain-specific instantiations, but the systemic aspects (data classification, consent architecture, threat modelling) will likely require new vocabulary.

### 11.2 BSMM extraction

The Business System Meta Model promotion — extracting implicit system meta model concepts from Foundation, Knowledge, ServiceDelivery, Platform, Operations, and PatternCatalogue into a named, navigable structure — is a recognised future workstream. The BMM provides the template for what this looks like: explicit vocabulary, comprehension metadata, weighted relationships, and console tooling.

### 11.3 Domain identity implementation

Domain identity as a first-class model concept ([[ontara-discussion-domain-identity-architecture-2026-03-22-v2.1|B15]]) — a `DomainDefinition` part def in Foundation with canonical properties (name, label, regulatory tier, BMM vocabulary scope, directory path, demonstrator status) — is a candidate near-term workstream. This would provide the registration record for each tenant instantiation under the multi-tenancy principle.

### 11.4 Ontological grounding

The BMM has been recognised as a de facto BFO-aligned service business mid-level ontology ([[ontara-discussion-ontological-grounding-2026-03-22|B18, B19]]). The three-layer ontology stack (BFO → CCO/OGMS/IAO → domain) provides a formal grounding for the BMM's concepts. This is a directional commitment — not yet implemented — but the current BMM vocabulary does not foreclose it ([[concept-non-constraining|J3]]).

### 11.5 Reasoning formalisms

The weighted relationship model (§4.2) supports three interpretive frames — costs/preferences, fuzzy human judgements, and probabilities. Three formal reasoning approaches have been identified as relevant: semiring soft-constraints, fuzzy multi-criteria decision-making, and Probabilistic Soft Logic. These remain a research direction (M7); current work preserves optionality ([[concept-non-constraining|J3]]).
