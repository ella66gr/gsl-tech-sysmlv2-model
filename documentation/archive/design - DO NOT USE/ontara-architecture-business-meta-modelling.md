## 1. Purpose and Intent

This document describes the Business Meta Model (BMM) of the [[ontara-project-map|Ontara]] platform — a structural vocabulary that captures what any service business *is*: its value proposition, financial logic, resource strategy, activity structure, governance obligations, stakeholder relationships, and strategic direction. The BMM is one half of a [[principle-two-meta-model-distinction|two meta model architecture (A4)]]; the other half is the System Meta Model (SMM), which captures how a business *system* works.

The BMM addresses three goals:

- **Make the business model explicit and visible** alongside the business system model, so that the two can be developed iteratively, with each informing the other.
- **Define a generic vocabulary** that captures the structural, functional, and dynamic features of a service business model — applicable to any business that sees itself primarily as serving customer need. Healthcare specifics arise only when a specific domain (such as GenderSense Limited) instantiates the generic abstractions.
- **Enable quantitative exploration** of business model variants through parameterised scenarios, financial projections, and operational steering — turning a structured description into an explorable, computable tool.

The two meta models are distinct but connected by explicit, visible mappings. A ServiceOffering in the business model maps to one or more service processes in the system model. A RevenueStream maps to billable events generated by the delivery system. A ResourceType maps to workforce, equipment, or platform elements. These mappings are where the iterative back-and-forth between business model and business system happens — and making them structural (rather than implicit) is a core benefit of the approach.

Under the [[concept-multi-tenancy|multi-tenancy principle (A13)]] — promoted to binding Tier 1 in Session 142 — only the meta model is core. Every domain — including the primary motivating use case, GenderSense Limited — is a tenant instantiation: an exercise of the platform's capabilities against a specific service business. Domain identity is now structurally expressed across SysML (`DomainIdentity` + `DomainConfiguration`, Session 143), OWL (`ontara-domain.ttl`, Session 144), and the generation pipeline — see [[ontara-discussion-domain-identity-dual-stack-2026-04-05|Domain Identity in the Dual-Stack Architecture (Session 142)]].

The intended benefits are: completeness checking (has every concern been addressed?), modular exploration (can I substitute this component and trace the consequences?), stress-testing (does this business model variant work financially and operationally?), and communicability (can I discuss this with a non-technical colleague, an investor, or a clinical advisor by pointing at a visible structure rather than narrating from memory?).

## 2. Conceptual Framework

### 2.1 The Six Concerns of a Service Business

Any service business, regardless of sector, must address six orthogonal but interconnected concerns. These are not layers or stages — they coexist and interact continuously. Each concern is realised as a named package within the BMM.

**Service Concept** (`BusinessModel::ServiceConcept`) — What value is delivered, to whom, and why it is worth paying for. This encompasses the value proposition, the customer segments served, the service offerings packaged for delivery, the channels through which customers are reached, the differentiation claims that distinguish the service from alternatives, and — critically — the identification of [[concept-service-subject|service subjects]] (what the service acts upon) and [[concept-service-participant|service participants]] (who else is involved, in what role). It answers: "why does this business exist, and for whom?"

**Activity Model** (`BusinessModel::ActivityModel`) — A cross-cutting foundation beneath and across all other concerns. The business must be aware of every unit of activity that occurs within it, whether that activity is system-implemented or human-implemented, whether it is directly billable or not. Activity is the common currency that connects the concerns: the Service Concept promises value; the delivery system produces it through activities; resources are consumed by activities; the Financial Model can only produce meaningful projections if it can see the full activity picture. Activity awareness is treated as a concern in its own right (rather than merely a cross-cutting theme) because it has its own vocabulary, its own progressive elaboration model, and its own planning and costing structures. See §2.4 for the full treatment.

**Resource Planning** (`BusinessModel::ResourcePlanning`) — What resources and capabilities are required to operate the service delivery system, and how they are acquired, developed, and allocated. Resources include people (by role and competence), estate and facilities, equipment, technology, licences, and external services. Capabilities are the organised combinations of resources that produce service outcomes. It answers: "what do we need in order to do what we do?"

**Financial Planning** (`BusinessModel::FinancialPlanning`) — How money flows through the business. Revenue streams (how income is generated from service delivery), cost structure (how resources consume money), unit economics (the relationship between a unit of service delivered and its financial impact), pricing models, and financial projections linked to scenarios. It answers: "does this work financially?"

**Governance Mapping** (`BusinessModel::GovernanceMapping`) — How governance obligations are tracked and evidenced. This concern provides the structural vocabulary for governance traceability: what the obligations are (GovernanceRequirement) and what records demonstrate compliance (AuditEvidenceRecord). The traceability chain runs from requirement through testable condition (domain-specific constraint def) through formal satisfaction link to proof of compliance. This concern was validated when the [[domain-suds|Suds]] demonstrator required COSHH governance traceability, confirming that [[principle-clinical-governance-first-class|governance as a first-class concern (A8)]] is not healthcare-specific but a general service business need. The [[ontara-discussion-deontic-governance-architecture-2026-04-03|deontic governance architecture]] (Sessions 121–131) extends this with a formal obligation vocabulary in OWL — see §11.2.

**Stakeholder Model** (`BusinessModel::StakeholderModel`) — How the business connects to, cooperates with, and jointly delivers value alongside external entities. The first five concerns are inward-facing — they describe the internal logic of the business. StakeholderModel captures the relational boundary: the typed, structured relationships the business maintains with regulators, partners, suppliers, communities, and the co-production role of the customer. Six General elements provide the vocabulary: [[concept-stakeholder-model|StakeholderRelationship]] (the core abstraction for typed external relationships), CooperativeArrangement (formalised joint delivery), ReferralPathway (structured routes for directing or receiving work), ExternalDependency (asymmetric reliance on things the business does not control), CommunityRelationship (constitutive connections to communities of interest), and ParticipationModel (how the customer participates as co-producer, not merely recipient). Regulatory relationships are dual-classified: rule content in GovernanceMapping, relationship structure in StakeholderModel, linked by typed `ref` (see §3.1). This concern was proposed in Session 76, detailed design completed in Session 78, and SysML implementation completed in Session 81. See the [[ontara-discussion-stakeholder-model-detailed-design-2026-03-28|StakeholderModel detailed design paper]]. It answers: "who do we interact with, how, and why does it matter?"

**Strategy and Evolution** (`BusinessStrategy`) — Where the business is heading. Strategic objectives with timeframes and success criteria, business model variants for exploration, and pivot scenarios for structured what-if analysis about transitioning between variants. This concern connects upward to enterprise strategy and downward to the scenario modelling mechanics that parameterise each variant.

The broader governance ecosystem — regulatory requirements (`Enterprise::Regulation`), governance processes (`ServiceDelivery::ClinicalGovernance`), risk registers, and learning mechanisms — spans both the BMM and the SMM. The GovernanceMapping concern within the BMM provides the general vocabulary for the traceability chain; domain-specific regulatory content and governance processes live in the system model packages where they are operationally managed. The deontic governance vocabulary in the knowledge graph (Sessions 121–131) provides the formal obligation vocabulary alongside this SysML-level traceability.

### 2.2 Relationships Between the Six Concerns

The concerns are not independent. The key structural relationships are:

- The **Service Concept** defines what the **delivery system** must produce and what **governance obligations** attach.
- The **delivery system** consumes **Resources and Capabilities** to operate, generating **activities** as it does so.
- **Resources and Capabilities** have costs, which flow into the **Financial Model**.
- **Activities** are the common currency: the Service Concept promises value; the delivery system produces it through activities; resources are consumed by activities; the Financial Model can only produce meaningful projections if it can see the full activity picture.
- **Governance Mapping** constrains all other concerns — regulatory limits on what can be offered, how it can be delivered, who can deliver it, and what can be charged. Governance obligations are HardConstraints in the reasoning metamodel's terms — they define NormativeRegion boundaries in the [[concept-coordinate-framework|coordinate space (A12)]], not suggestions to be weighed.
- The **Financial Model** constrains what **Resources and Capabilities** can be acquired, which constrains what the delivery system can do, which constrains what **Service Concept** can be realised.
- **Strategy and Evolution** provides the intentional direction that the other concerns serve, expressed as strategic objectives that trace through to the capabilities required to achieve them.
- **Stakeholder Model** connects outward to the entities the business interacts with. Regulatory stakeholder relationships link to **Governance Mapping** through typed references. Cooperative arrangements and referral pathways affect what **Service Concept** can be delivered and what **Activities** are generated. External dependencies constrain **Resources and Capabilities**. ParticipationModel connects back to **Service Concept** through typed references to ServiceOffering. Community relationships shape the business's identity and service design.

This creates a circular dependency that is not a defect — it is the reality of running a business. The BMM makes the circle visible and navigable rather than implicit and surprising. The 96 [[concept-weighted-relationships|weighted relationships]] (§4.2) quantify the strength of these inter-concern dependencies.

### 2.3 Modularity Principle

Each concern is decomposed into components. Components have defined interfaces (what they require from and provide to other components), substitutability (a component can be replaced with an alternative that satisfies the same interfaces, allowing exploration of business model variants), and varying levels of coupling — loose coupling at higher abstraction levels (the Service Concept does not dictate platform technology choices), tighter coupling at lower levels (a specific clinical pathway requires specific clinical competencies).

This is the "loose fit at high abstraction, tight fit at low levels" principle. The BMM defines the interface contracts; the concrete model implements them.

### 2.4 Activity Awareness — A Cross-Cutting Foundation

Beneath and across all six concerns lies a foundational requirement: the business must be aware of every unit of activity that occurs within it, whether that activity is system-implemented or human-implemented, whether it is directly billable or not.

This matters because activity is the common currency that connects the concerns. If any category of activity is invisible, the financial model underestimates cost, the resource model underestimates demand, and the capacity model produces false confidence.

**Activity taxonomy.** Every activity in the business falls into one of five categories: service delivery activity (directly produces value for a customer), service-enabling activity (supports service delivery but is not directly visible to the customer), governance activity (satisfies compliance, quality, and safety requirements), development activity (builds future capability), and overhead activity (necessary operational activity that is neither directly value-producing nor capability-building). These categories are not rigid boundaries — a single activity might serve multiple purposes. The taxonomy exists to ensure completeness of coverage, not to force artificial classification.

**Progressive elaboration.** Activity awareness operates at multiple levels of granularity, and different parts of the business model and system model can operate at different levels simultaneously: envelope level (total activity hours per category estimated as a lump), category level (activity broken down by type with approximate proportions and durations), and tracked level (individual activities recorded as discrete events with participants, duration, resource consumption, and category classification). Different parts of the business can operate at different granularity levels at the same time. Over time, granularity increases where it adds value.

**Stakeholder relationships as activity generators.** The [[concept-stakeholder-model|StakeholderModel]] concern generates activity across multiple categories. Regulatory relationship reviews and compliance reporting are governance activity. Referral processing and cooperative delivery coordination are service-enabling activity. Community engagement and partnership development are development activity. These activities consume resources and carry costs — making them visible through the activity taxonomy ensures they are not treated as invisible overhead.

## 3. The Business Meta Model Vocabulary

The BMM vocabulary is defined in SysML v2 as `part def` and `requirement def` elements, each annotated with comprehension metadata (§4) and BFO classification (§4.1). The vocabulary is distributed across three SysML files to manage file size, but logically forms a single coherent model. Domain identity infrastructure lives in a fourth location (`Foundation::DomainRegistry`, Session 143).

### 3.1 Core elements (34, in `BusinessModel`)

The core BMM package contains 34 elements across six concern packages. All 34 are classified as General — common to most service businesses, sector-agnostic. No Tailored (sector-specific) BMM elements have been introduced to date; the current vocabulary has proven sufficient to model four structurally different service domains.

**Service Concept** (9 elements):

| Element | Type | Description |
|---|---|---|
| CustomerSegment | part def | A defined group of customers with shared needs and willingness to pay |
| ValueProposition | part def | A promise of value to a customer segment — what they get and why it matters |
| ServiceOffering | part def | A packaged unit of service that can be priced, resourced, and delivered |
| Channel | part def | A way that customers find, reach, or receive the service |
| DifferentiationClaim | part def | An explicit, testable statement of how the service differs from alternatives |
| ServiceSubject | part def | The entity upon which a service is performed — person, animal, object, or organisation |
| ServiceParticipant | part def | An entity involved in a service engagement in a defined role |
| CatalogueEntry | part def | A record of something the business has decided to offer, with price and status |
| ExternalReference | part def | A link to authoritative knowledge that lives outside the system |

ServiceSubject and ServiceParticipant were introduced in Session 55 to resolve a structural observation from the Paws demonstrator: the entity upon which a service is performed (the dog being groomed) is not always the customer (the pet owner). These are sibling concepts, not a hierarchy — either can independently trigger workflow. Cross-domain validation confirms the distinction: Paws (dog/owner), GSL (patient/commissioner/carer), Suds (garments/customer), Cafe (customer is also the subject — a valid trivial case).

**Activity Model** (5 elements):

| Element | Type | Description |
|---|---|---|
| ActivityType | part def | A defined kind of business activity, classified into one of five categories |
| ActivityRecord | part def | A recorded instance of an activity that actually happened |
| ActivityBudget | part def | A planned allocation of activity volume by type and period |
| ActivityGranularity | part def | A policy declaration of how precisely activity is tracked in a given area |
| ActivityCostAllocation | part def | The mapping from an activity type to its financial cost |

**Resource Planning** (7 elements):

| Element | Type | Description |
|---|---|---|
| ResourceType | part def | A category of resource needed to run the service |
| ResourceInstance | part def | A specific resource available at a defined capacity and cost |
| Capability | part def | An organised combination of resources that produces a defined service function |
| CapacityModel | part def | The relationship between resource configuration and service throughput |
| ResourceConstraint | part def | A limit on resource availability that affects service delivery |
| InventoryRecord | part def | A point-in-time record of stock level for a catalogue item |
| ObjectiveCapabilityMapping | part def | A declared connection between a strategic objective and supporting capabilities |

**Financial Planning** (5 elements):

| Element | Type | Description |
|---|---|---|
| RevenueStream | part def | A mechanism by which the business generates income from its services |
| CostDriver | part def | A structural factor that generates cost — linked to a resource or activity |
| UnitEconomics | part def | The revenue, cost, and margin for a single unit of service delivery |
| PricingModel | part def | How prices are set — per-episode, subscription, tiered, or other basis |
| FinancialProjection | part def | A forward-looking financial picture linked to a scenario definition |

**Governance Mapping** (2 elements):

| Element | Type | Description |
|---|---|---|
| GovernanceRequirement | requirement def | A regulatory or standards obligation that the business must satisfy |
| AuditEvidenceRecord | part def | A record that demonstrates compliance with a governance requirement |

GovernanceRequirement is modelled as a `requirement def` (not `part def`) to enable formal `satisfy` relationships to domain-specific constraint usages. The traceability chain: GovernanceRequirement → constraint def → satisfy → AuditEvidenceRecord. This chain has been validated in the Suds demonstrator's COSHH governance model and extended into the knowledge graph through the deontic governance vocabulary (Sessions 121–131).

**Stakeholder Model** (6 elements):

| Element | Type | Description |
|---|---|---|
| StakeholderRelationship | part def | A typed, ongoing, structured relationship between the business and an external entity. Carries `ref relatedGovernanceRequirements : GovernanceRequirement` |
| CooperativeArrangement | part def | A formalised agreement to jointly deliver or manage something — distinguished by `sharedOutcome` |
| ReferralPathway | part def | A structured route for directing or receiving work between organisations. `direction : ReferralDirection` |
| ExternalDependency | part def | Something the business depends on that it does not control — asymmetric reliance with `criticality : DependencyCriticality` |
| CommunityRelationship | part def | The business's connection to its broader community of interest — constitutive, not transactional |
| ParticipationModel | part def | How the customer/patient participates as co-producer, not merely recipient. Carries `ref relatedServiceOffering : ServiceOffering` |

## 4. The Comprehension Architecture and the BMM

The BMM is not only a structural vocabulary — it is a self-describing vocabulary. Every element carries comprehension metadata that enables the system to explain what it contains, why it is structured that way, and how elements relate to each other.

### 4.1 Self-describing elements

Every one of the 34 core BMM elements carries six annotation types:

- **`@CatalogueTag`** — identifies the element's BMM concern and General/Tailored classification. Used by the generation pipeline.
- **`@UserFacing`** — a friendly name and short description for non-technical users.
- **`@PurposiveDescription`** — a human-written explanation of why the element exists and what it does for the service business. Register 1 in the three-register comprehension model.
- **`@Comprehension`** — four boolean flags that tell the generation pipeline's traversal discovery engine which structural facts to dynamically derive. Register 2.
- **`@WeightedRelationship`** — directed, non-commutative strength of interaction between elements. 96 relationships across 33 weighted elements.
- **`@BfoType`** — BFO 2020 category, mid-level ontology parent (CCO/IAO), and classification justification. Applied Session 99. The SysML-side input to the OWL pipeline — `gen_owl_pipeline.py` reads `@BfoType` to generate correctly parented OWL classes in the knowledge graph.

Coverage is complete: 34/34 for all six annotation types.

### 4.2 Weighted relationships

96 `@WeightedRelationship` annotations express the strength of interaction between BMM concepts. Key properties: directional and non-commutative (the weight on A → B and B → A are independently assessed); AuditEvidenceRecord is a pure receiver with zero outgoing weights; every weight carries a rationale; five inductively established heuristics (H1–H5) govern assignment (see [[ontara-ref-weighted-relationship-heuristics-and-config|heuristics and configuration reference]]).

The weight model supports three interpretive frames — costs/preferences, fuzzy human judgements, and probabilities — formalised in the reasoning metamodel as named individuals (`ProbabilityFrame`, `FuzzyMembershipFrame`, `PreferenceWeightFrame`), stable since Session 46.

The [[principle-unity-principle|unity principle (A11)]] commits the platform to a single weighted relationship model informing comprehension, reasoning, simulation, governance, and assembly guidance. This commitment was empirically validated by the comprehension–reasoning convergence (S147-D7): Session 147 confirmed that the inferential register and the evidence architecture are the same underlying pattern.

Weighted relationships now have OWL representation: 96 reified individuals in `ontara-bmm-weights.ttl` (702 triples), pipeline-generated from the SysML annotations.

### 4.3 The three-register model

| Register | Content | Source | Status |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Register 1: Authored** | Human-written purposive descriptions | `@PurposiveDescription` metadata | Complete. 34/34 coverage. |
| **Register 2: Structural** | Facts the model already knows — type, relationships, containment, patterns, domain instantiations | Dynamically derived via `@Comprehension` traversal schema | Complete. 34/34 coverage. |
| **Register 2+: Inferential** | Derived explanations beyond what any single element states — analogies, gap analysis, impact propagation | Converged with the reasoning metamodel's evidence architecture (S147-D7): the SEPIO+PROV-O evidence architecture in `ontara-reasoning.ttl` provides the OWL infrastructure for Claims, EvidenceLines, and ConfidenceAssessments | Vocabulary implemented. Runtime engines are future work. |

The [[principle-intrinsic-self-knowledge|intrinsic self-knowledge principle (A10)]] governs the boundary: if the model changes and no human edits a description, does the explanation become wrong? If yes, that content must be intrinsic.

### 4.4 The BMM in the Ontara Console

The [[concept-co-evolution|co-evolution principle (J2)]] requires that modelling and tooling advance together. The BMM vocabulary is made legible through multiple console views: Coverage Matrix (which concepts are instantiated in which domains), Component Catalogue (four-quadrant classification with domain status and BFO category badges), Glossary (authored + intrinsic comprehension content, weight-aware related concepts with warm-to-cool dot bar), Governance view (traceability chain), Weighted Relationship Graph (interactive 3D WebGL of the 96 relationships), and Ontology view (BFO→CCO/IAO→BMM hierarchy with KG Status panel).

All views are generated from `model-introspection.json`, itself generated from the SysML model by `gen_model_introspection.py`. This is the comprehension architecture in practice: the model describes itself, the generator extracts the descriptions, and the console renders them for human navigation.

## 5. Cross-Domain Validation

### 5.1 The demonstrator strategy

Demonstrator domains serve two purposes ([[concept-cross-domain-validation|J1]]): cross-domain validation (proving that the BMM vocabulary generalises) and pedagogical anchoring (concrete illustrations that make abstract concepts tangible).

### 5.2 The domains

| Domain | Character | Key structural difference | Regulatory tier | BMM vocabulary scope |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| [[domain-cafe\|Cafe]] | Immediate retail | Per-item pricing, walk-in, 2-minute cycle | Generally governed | Full model + running application (22 validated patterns). StakeholderModel: 6 instantiations |
| [[domain-suds\|Suds]] | Batch processing | Weight/type-dependent pricing, batch turnaround, item tracking | Lightly regulated | Full BMM + COSHH governance traceability chain. StakeholderModel: 6 instantiations (Session 108) |
| [[domain-paws\|Paws]] | Appointment-based personal service | Per-appointment pricing, breed/size surcharges, persistent client/animal identity | Lightly regulated | General vocabulary + StakeholderModel: 7 instantiations |
| [[domain-ears\|Ears]] | Community ear care | Simple procedural pathway, clinical governance | Sector-regulated | Outlined (Session 97). Exercises all six BMM concerns and OGMS clinical primitives |

GenderSense Limited (GSL) is the fifth domain and the primary motivating use case. Under [[concept-multi-tenancy|A13]], GSL is the most important tenant — but still a tenant. Its distinction is purpose (production healthcare delivery) and regulatory tier (sector-regulated), not structural privilege.

### 5.3 Validation findings

The cross-domain validation programme has produced several concrete findings:

- **All General vocabulary has proved sufficient.** Four structurally different service domains have been modelled without requiring Tailored (sector-specific) BMM extensions.
- **ServiceSubject and ServiceParticipant generalise.** The Paws demonstrator exposed a gap, resolved by introducing two sibling concepts that generalise cleanly across all four domains.
- **GovernanceMapping generalises.** The COSHH governance traceability chain in Suds confirmed that the GovernanceRequirement → constraint → satisfy → AuditEvidenceRecord pattern works for any regulatory regime. This was further validated by the CQC Governance MVP (Session 131), which formalised CQC Regulation 12 with 21 individuals in the knowledge graph.
- **The four-layer item model generalises.** CatalogueEntry/ExternalReference/InventoryRecord validated across coffee menus, laundry item catalogues, and clinical formularies.
- **The four-tier regulatory classification works.** Generally governed (Cafe), lightly regulated (Paws, Suds), partially regulated (not yet instantiated), and sector-regulated (GSL, Ears) provide a clean discriminator.
- **StakeholderModel generalises.** All six elements have concrete content across all four demonstrator domains. 20 domain instantiations total: GSL (7), Cafe (6), Paws (7). The Suds gap was closed in Session 108 (6 instantiations).
- **Reasoning metamodel validates cross-domain.** The reasoning metamodel (Stage 7 Phase 1) achieved 24/24 cross-domain validation against Cafe and Suds, confirming that reasoning vocabulary is not domain-specific.

## 6. Package Structure and the SysML Model

### 6.1 Files and packages

The BMM vocabulary is distributed across SysML files:

| File | Package | Contents |
|---|---|---|
| `model/business-model.sysml` | `BusinessModel` | ServiceConcept, ActivityModel, ResourcePlanning, FinancialPlanning, GovernanceMapping, StakeholderModel. 34 elements. |
| `model/business-scenarios.sysml` | `BusinessScenarios` | Projection mechanics. 11 elements. |
| `model/business-strategy.sysml` | `BusinessStrategy` | Strategic direction. 3 elements. |
| `model/foundation.sysml` | `Foundation::DomainRegistry` | DomainIdentity, DomainConfiguration (Session 143). |

`BusinessScenarios` and `BusinessStrategy` are separate top-level packages (not sub-packages of `BusinessModel`) due to a Syside constraint.

### 6.2 Cross-references between packages

The BMM packages reference each other through typed `ref` attributes (migrated from String in Session 58): `ScenarioDefinition.activeServiceOfferings → ServiceOffering`, `FinancialProjection.scenario → ScenarioDefinition`, `Capability.enabledServiceOfferings → ServiceOffering`, `InventoryRecord.catalogueEntry → CatalogueEntry`, `CostDriver.linkedResource → ResourceType`, `StakeholderRelationship.relatedGovernanceRequirements → GovernanceRequirement`, `ParticipationModel.relatedServiceOffering → ServiceOffering`, and others. These typed references strengthen structural traceability and enable the OWL pipeline to generate 14 object properties from SysML typed `ref` attributes.

### 6.3 Relationship to Operations

There is an intentional division of responsibility between the BMM's financial and resource concerns and the Operations package. `BusinessModel::FinancialPlanning` addresses structural economics (how does this business model work financially?); `Operations::Finance` addresses operational accounting (invoice processing, payment reconciliation). `BusinessModel::ResourcePlanning` addresses structural resource needs; `Operations::People` addresses operational HR. The BMM asks "what does the business model require?"; Operations asks "how are those requirements managed operationally?" This mirrors the BMM/SMM distinction at the concern level.

## 8. Business Model Variants and Scenario Modelling

### 8.1 Three business model variants

Three variants are defined for the GSL tenant:

**Variant A: "Lean Clinical"** — Assessment and hormone therapy only. One prescribing clinician (0.5 FTE stepping to 0.8), one admin (0.3 FTE). Per-episode pricing. Break-even target at ~50 active patients, estimated around month 14. Fully elaborated.

**Variant B: "Full Platform"** — Assessment, hormone therapy, coaching, education, community, patient portal. Higher investment (~£120K), longer break-even, higher revenue ceiling. Target 200 active patients within 18 months. Fully elaborated.

**Variant C: "Consultancy + Platform Licence"** — Dual revenue: clinical services AND platform licence/SaaS. Highest investment (~£200K), platform revenue scalable independently of clinical capacity. Defined at summary level; deferred.

### 8.2 Projection mechanics

The scenario modelling vocabulary provides the mechanics for computing projections, comparing alternatives, and stress-testing assumptions. ScenarioDefinition selects active service offerings and configures parameters. GrowthAssumption defines time-parameterised trajectories (linear, stepped, S-curve, custom). ProjectionFormula defines evaluable expressions. SensitivityParameter identifies which assumptions the model most depends on — for the Lean Clinical variant, patient acquisition rate is the dominant variable.

### 8.3 Operational steering

Once operational, the projection becomes a living instrument through the forecast–actuals–rebaseline cycle: establish baseline → record actuals → analyse variance → update forward forecast → re-baseline when assumptions are materially wrong. Three views are always available: original plan, current forecast, and actuals.

This cycle is structurally parallel to the self-knowledge architecture's [[pattern-five-layer-self-knowledge|five-layer SystemStateAssessment]]: GoalProjector parallels ProjectionFormulas; OperationalStateAggregator parallels financial actuals queries; GapAnalyser parallels VarianceAnalysis. Implementing operational steering is primarily an extension of existing infrastructure.

## 9. The Two Meta Models

### 9.1 Current state

The [[principle-two-meta-model-distinction|two meta model distinction (A4)]] is the load-bearing architectural commitment that separates *what a service business is* from *how a business system works*.

The **Business Meta Model** is explicitly modelled (50 elements including domain identity, this document) and navigable through the Ontara Console. Its vocabulary, comprehension metadata, weighted relationships, and BFO classifications form a self-describing, ontologically grounded structural vocabulary for service business modelling.

The **System Meta Model (SMM)** has grown substantially since its concepts were first identified. Explicit SMM-side content now includes: [[concept-architectural-section|ArchitecturalSection]] (B27, Session 87) — 1 `part def`, 20 `part` usages encoding the dual-stack architecture; `DomainConfiguration` (Session 143) — system settings for domain identity; the deontic governance vocabulary (`ontara-governance.ttl`, Sessions 121–131) — 19 OWL classes for formal obligation representation; the domain identity vocabulary (`ontara-domain.ttl`, Session 144); the reasoning metamodel vocabulary (`ontara-reasoning.ttl`, Sessions 150–152) — 26 OWL classes for institutionalised reasoning; and the PROV-O core subset (`prov-core.ttl`, Session 150). A future workstream will promote the remaining implicit SMM concepts distributed across Foundation, Knowledge, ServiceDelivery, Platform, and Operations into a named, navigable package structure (gap O2).

### 9.2 The mapping structure

The two meta models are connected by explicit horizontal mappings at every tier (B12). Domain-specific models instantiate both meta models. A ServiceOffering in the business model maps to one or more ServiceProcesses in the system model. A RevenueStream maps to billable events generated by the delivery system. A GovernanceRequirement maps to deontic directives in the knowledge graph and to domain-specific constraint defs.

### 9.3 The General/Tailored decomposition

Within each meta model, components are classified as General (sector-agnostic) or Tailored (sector-specific). Currently, all 50 BMM elements are General — no Tailored extensions have been needed. The [[concept-general-tailored-decomposition|B11]] decomposition provides for sector-specific extensions via `:>>` redefinition.

## 10. Simulation: Practising the Business Before Running It

The BMM's structural vocabulary, combined with the scenario modelling mechanics and the system model's pathway and workflow infrastructure, brings a simulation capability within architectural reach. Simulation differs fundamentally from projection: a projection aggregates ("at month 12, you'll have approximately 50 patients generating approximately £X revenue"), while a simulation instantiates — generating individual simulated patients, each flowing through the pathway model, consuming specific resources, encountering specific decision points, and revealing emergent behaviour that aggregation hides.

The simulation architecture (Session 73) establishes four layered capabilities: the [[concept-operational-simulation|operational simulation (L5)]] as the SMM made live, the [[concept-reflective-simulation|reflective simulation (L6)]] as a cross-cutting advisory capability, [[concept-valence|valence (L7)]] as the operator's evaluative criteria, and [[concept-coordinate-space-snapshots|coordinate space snapshots (L8)]] persisting multiple epistemic states. [[concept-goal-seeking-computation|Goal-seeking computation (L9)]] — reinterpreted by Session 147 as pathfinding through constrained coordinate space — searches for action sequences moving the business from current state to goal state.

Key design concerns for simulation include: simulation data generation (patient generation, event generation, environmental starter sets), workflow execution under simulation (automated progression, decision point resolution, resource contention), temporal control (time compression, checkpointing for branching), simulation reporting (same data structures as the live system), and stakeholder dynamics (referral pathway volumes as time-varying inputs, external dependency disruption scenarios, cooperative arrangement performance).

## 11. The BMM in the Knowledge Graph

The BMM has a dual representation: SysML v2 as the structural source of truth, and OWL 2 DL in the knowledge graph as the ontological representation. The OWL pipeline (`gen_owl_pipeline.py`) translates between them via declarative mapping rules, and three layers of automated quality assurance verify fidelity.

### 11.1 OWL representation

The BMM's OWL representation, pipeline-generated from SysML, comprises: 34 OWL classes (one per BMM element, correctly parented via `@BfoType`), 14 object properties (generated from SysML typed `ref` attributes with domain, range, and functional characteristics), 96 reified weighted relationship individuals (702 triples), and 1,378 correspondence triples (mapping records linking SysML elements to OWL counterparts with provenance). Hand-authored axioms in `ontara-bmm-axioms.ttl` add 6 concern-group disjointness declarations and 9 qualified cardinality restrictions.

### 11.2 Governance extensions

The [[ontara-discussion-deontic-governance-architecture-2026-04-03|deontic governance vocabulary]] (Sessions 121–131) extends the BMM's GovernanceMapping concern into the knowledge graph with formal semantic grounding. Four deontic modalities (Obligation, Prohibition, Permission, RegulatoryPower), a normative instrument taxonomy (B33), and GovernanceFramework (B31) provide the vocabulary for making governance requirements machine-readable and computationally active. The CQC Governance MVP (Session 131) demonstrated this with 21 individuals formalising CQC Regulation 12. Governance obligations are HardConstraints in the reasoning metamodel's terms — they define NormativeRegion boundaries in the [[concept-coordinate-framework|coordinate space (A12)]].

### 11.3 Quality assurance

Three layers of automated QA verify the BMM's OWL representation: SPARQL validation (43 queries in 11 groups), OWL 2 DL consistency checking (HermiT via Robot, 12-file ontology stack), and round-trip diff (288 semantic units, 0 discrepancies). See the [[ontara-ref-vision-architecture|Vision and Architecture Reference (v9)]] §5 for full detail.

## 12. Forward Direction

### 12.1 Information governance and cybersecurity (B20)

IG and cybersecurity have been identified as a cross-cutting concern requiring representation in both meta models ([[ontara-workflow-emergent-ideas-log|E011]]). The current vocabulary can accommodate IG-specific governance requirements as domain-specific instantiations, but systemic aspects (data classification, consent architecture, threat modelling) will likely require new vocabulary. Partially subsumed by the governance workstream (B30–B35). Observations on IG, pseudonymisation, and BI captured in E023–E025 (Session 147).

### 12.2 SMM extraction

Promoting implicit system meta model concepts into a named, navigable structure — is a recognised future workstream (gap O2). The BMM provides the template.

### 12.3 Tailored StakeholderModel extensions

Healthcare-specific Tailored extensions have been identified as candidates for the GSL instantiation: `SharedCareProtocol` (extending CooperativeArrangement), `ClinicalReferralPathway` (extending ReferralPathway), and `PatientAdvocacyRelationship` (extending CommunityRelationship). Deferred to GSL instantiation scoping. See the [[ontara-discussion-stakeholder-model-detailed-design-2026-03-28|detailed design paper]] §7.

### 12.4 Ears demonstrator

The [[domain-ears|Ears]] community ear care demonstrator (outlined Session 97) is the fifth demonstrator domain and the second clinical domain. It exercises all six BMM concerns and OGMS clinical primitives, providing the first sector-regulated cross-domain validation. Build-out tracked as [[ontara-ref-work-items|W-015]].

### 12.5 Governance activation tier

Stage 6 Block B — connecting the governance framework library to specific tenant service models through obligation binding. This would make the deontic governance vocabulary operationally active: obligations from a governance framework bound to specific BMM elements in a tenant's business model.

### 12.6 Stage 7 Phase 2 — Reasoning depth

Heuristic packs, decision mode routing (Cynefin domain mapping), and constraint satisfaction structures — extending the reasoning metamodel's abstract types into operational depth. The BMM's weighted relationships connect to constraint satisfaction through the [[principle-unity-principle|unity principle (A11)]].
